# ReturnWindow: where the money actually is

Written 8 September 2026. Honest about what is proven and what is a guess.

## What is true today

The tool is live at https://harsh01369.github.io/returnwindow/ and it works.
It has no users, no traffic and no revenue. Nothing below has been validated by
a single real customer yet. Treat every number here as a hypothesis.

## Why this audience is worth serving

The buyer is an Indian professional abroad, planning a return. They earn in
USD, GBP or AED, so they price like a Western customer rather than an Indian
one. That is the same "West plus diaspora" thesis already in the growth policy,
pointed at a different need.

The decision is high-stakes and time-boxed. Someone with a UK pension, vested
RSUs or a US brokerage account can have a six-figure sum sitting on the wrong
side of a date. Against that, a few hundred pounds for certainty is cheap. That
is a much better willingness-to-pay profile than a subscription sold into India.

## Why we can win a crowded space

Competitors exist: desireturn, returningnris, deshwaapsi, backtoindia. They ship
checklists, blog posts and single-point "what is my status this year"
calculators. None of them computes the thing that actually decides the outcome.

The 729-day limb of s.6(6)(a) looks seven years back from a year that has not
happened yet. It routinely puts the decisive date somewhere nobody would look.
In the worked case in the test suite it lands on 12 July: arrive on the 11th and
you lose an entire year of shelter. A status checker cannot surface that, because
it is not a fact about this year.

This is the same bet that worked for Bhairav Aaradhyaa. The spiritual content
space was saturated too. Rigour won because everyone else was shallow.

## The monetisation ladder

**L1, free forever.** The calculator. It is the SEO asset and the trust asset.
Never gate it, never ask for an email before showing the answer.

**L2, the paid artefact (build next).** People will not pay for a number. They
will pay for something they can act on and hand to somebody else:

- a dated deadline calendar covering the FEMA account redesignation on return,
  the ToR shipping window, and the tax filing dates for each projected year;
- a written record of the day counts and the limb relied on for each year, in a
  form a chartered accountant can check in ten minutes rather than two hours;
- the interpretive flags stated plainly, so the CA argues the point rather than
  discovering it.

Price it in the currency the buyer earns in. £39 to £79 is defensible for a
decision worth lakhs. Sell it as a one-off, not a subscription; this is a
once-in-a-lifetime transaction and a subscription would be dishonest.

**L3, referral.** Once there is traffic, the natural partners are CA firms doing
NRI returns, currency transfer, and international movers. Lead value in this
segment is real money, materially more than display advertising would pay.
Do this only with firms whose work we would stand behind. A bad referral costs
more trust than the fee is worth.

**Not doing:** display advertising on a page where people type their income and
travel history. It would contradict the privacy promise that makes the tool
credible.

## Distribution, in the order to attempt it

1. **The technical story.** "One day in July costs you a year of Indian tax" is
   a genuinely good Hacker News post, because it is really about date
   arithmetic being load-bearing in law. It reaches builders, not buyers, but it
   earns the first inbound links, and links are what make the SEO work later.
2. **Where returnees already ask.** r/nri, r/india, the returning-NRI Facebook
   groups, Quora threads on RNOR. Answer the actual question in the thread,
   properly, and link the tool as working. Do not drop bare links.
3. **SEO, the compounding engine.** The head terms are contested, but the
   valuable queries are long-tail and specific: "when should I return to India
   for tax purposes", "RNOR how many years", "does the year I move back count as
   a visit". Each deserves a real page that answers it and cites the statute.
   This is the same play that took Bhairav from 448 to 976 clicks in six weeks.
4. **The CA channel.** Practitioners who do this work repeatedly are the fastest
   route to trust, because their clients ask them this question constantly.

## What could sink this

- **Getting the law wrong.** Trust is the entire product. Every rate and rule
  needs a date stamp and a primary source. Where sources conflict, say so, as
  the page already does for the ToR allowance.
- **Rule drift.** Section 6 is stable, but the Finance Act moves thresholds. The
  logic is evergreen; the numbers need an annual review each February.
- **Advice liability.** The page says clearly that this is not tax advice. Keep
  it that way. Sell certainty about dates, never an assurance about outcomes.
